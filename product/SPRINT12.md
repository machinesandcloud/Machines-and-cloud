## Operating bounds before rollout

| Path | Current bound and implication |
|---|---|
| Number acquisition | One operation per ten-minute scheduled run, at most 15 seconds. Number reservation is a separate outcome from service activation. Its execution gate remains disabled. |
| Voice | At most 25 retained active operations; the 26th admission is rejected before resource creation. A wave is at most four minutes with a 15-second budget per customer. Routing older than 25 minutes cannot establish readiness. Per-customer and account daily call/minute limits require an explicitly approved model/usage policy; no paid rates or limits were invented. |
| Messaging registration | One scheduled customer operation, at most three checkpoints in 15 seconds; customer-triggered continuation uses the same durable work. Approval/routing proof expires after 24 hours. Health inspects at most 50 records and marks a larger or overdue scope unhealthy. This is not a proven 50-customer throughput promise. |
| Calendar | At most 50 eligible jobs and 200 source discoveries within a two-minute wave, with at most 12 seconds per provider job. A desired version has at most five attempts. Broad retained-job scans and failure census still need archive/index work and measured scale acceptance. |
| Message delivery | The pending index retains at most 500 entries. A two-minute wave runs at most 12 short batches; an individual run has a deadline of at most 15 seconds. Full/uncertain work remains visible, rather than being silently dropped or resent. This limit is not a demonstrated live send rate. |
| Operator service checks | At most three dispatch attempts with retained failure health; the scheduler fairly processes one request by default. A dispatched check is not a successful service receipt. |

Worker leases and receipt freshness are local correctness controls. They do not establish provider latency, outage availability or capacity at the limits above. Expand and test these bounds before a broader rollout.
