# Customer and operations portals

October 22, 2026. UI/UX engineering and Product Owner working specification.

The customer wants to set up a front desk once, understand what is ready, finish the few steps that need their permission, and then manage real customer work. The founder needs to see every account, spot problems before a customer reports them, inspect the evidence, and resolve an issue without editing storage or borrowing an owner credential.

## Research and decisions

The current public [Intercom Inbox guide](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/6258745-the-inbox-explained) combines a filterable queue with customer context and a detail preview. Its useful lesson here is to keep the issue and account evidence together. This does not establish that Machines & Cloud has Intercom's messaging, assignment or presence features.

[Stripe's event guide](https://docs.stripe.com/development/dashboard/events) shows event activity and filters, and directs new accounts to Workbench. We will retain the actual operation, time and result of a support action. A visible retry is useful only when the underlying operation is durable and safe to repeat; a button must not imply that a provider succeeded.

[Jobber Client Hub](https://www.getjobber.com/features/client-hub/) groups immediate customer jobs such as requests and appointment details. Our portal should similarly prioritize setup progress and the work received by the front desk, with rules and account settings secondary. This is a pattern reference, not a claim that our portal supports Jobber payments, quoting or native integration.

These sources were reviewed on October 22, 2026. The authenticated competitor products were not exercised.

## Founder portal

Navigation starts with Overview, Customers, Issues, Activity, Inquiries and System. On a small screen these remain reachable without a narrow multicolumn table. Customer search matches business name, owner contact and account identifier. Filters use the actual saved service and onboarding state. Selecting a customer opens one detail view with identity, current connections, checks, usage, recent activity and available support actions.

The first screen answers: what needs attention, which customers are affected, and when was this information checked? An empty result, an unavailable service and a successful zero count must be visibly different. Load failures preserve the last confirmed data with an explicit stale warning; signing out or changing credentials immediately clears private views and invalidates old responses.

Support notes are internal and labeled. Customer changes require a reason and show the resulting saved state. Read actions cannot send a report, buy a number, start a call or change billing. Destructive or external actions must state the scope and consequences before submission. The portal does not expose customer owner keys, provider secrets or unrestricted impersonation links.

## Customer portal

The customer sees one setup journey. Each requested connection has a real state: waiting for the customer, preparing, pending provider approval, ready, or needs attention. The page names the next useful action rather than presenting implementation details. Saving a preference is never displayed as activation. Background work has a last-updated time and a recoverable result; unfinished provider automation stays visibly unfinished.

Once useful service is ready, daily work takes priority: new requests, bookings, conversations and availability. Setup remains accessible. A short contextual walkthrough explains the current page, can be dismissed and reopened, and never blocks urgent work. All actions need loading, success, failure, retry and sign-in-expiry states, with keyboard focus moved only when the user requests navigation.

## Acceptance boundaries

The implementation must be exercised with actual application handlers and isolated storage for the complete local journey. Browser fixtures establish layout and recovery behavior separately. QA must cover owner versus admin authority, no secret-bearing links, stale-response fencing, duplicate actions, unavailable storage, empty accounts, mobile overflow, keyboard operation and honest channel status. External email delivery, provider provisioning and hosted operation require their own evidence; local progress cards do not establish them.

## Implemented interaction and engineering evidence

`admin.html`, `admin.js` and `admin.css` now provide the dedicated operator portal. Its mailbox link is consumed only after an explicit Continue. The eight-hour operator session is separate from customer access and kept under a separate browser session key. The former shared-key entry and credential-bearing customer links are absent. Sign out immediately clears the private view and pending responses while a separate server revocation request completes.

The portal has Overview, Customers, Issues, Activity, Inquiries and System. Customer pages are loaded in bounded groups; the overview, search and issue counts explicitly cover the accounts loaded so far. Selecting an account requests its current details. The detail view combines current connections, setup checks, background work, bounded customer records, internal notes and support actions. Legacy accounts are read only. Supported customer corrections currently cover services, service area, excluded areas and saved business instructions. Each support action carries a reason and the expected server revision. A stale submission requires closing the dialog and reviewing the new account state before starting again.

When a customer change is confirmed saved but the optional detail projection cannot be returned, the UI clears its old account cache and offers **Refresh customer details**. It does not resubmit the accepted change. Failed ordinary refreshes retain the last confirmed view with a visible stale warning. CSV exports require an explicit action; the marketing and all-inquiry lists remain separate. Neither export sends mail.

The customer portal's Setup page now has **Workspace updates**. It shows durable refresh state, update time, operation reference and a server-authorized retry when available. The owner can explicitly request a refresh from saved business answers or read the latest status. Page view does not queue work. This job does not activate website chat or provision phone, texting or Calendar.

## Gated number request: current local acceptance

The owner portal has a conditional number-request card. With execution off and no saved request, the card and purchase form are absent; the existing connections still explain that phone answering is unavailable. An approved, current number-only offer can be presented only to a testing or live customer workspace. The authority statement comes from that offer. Authority and disclosed costs require separate, unchecked confirmations and an explicit submit. Saving connection preferences does not accept either. A number request never claims to activate answering, customer texts, routing or a calendar.

Existing requests remain visible when execution is disabled or the current offer disappears. The owner can explicitly confirm cancellation. Its dialog explains that a reserved number needs confirmed release and that the resource receipt stays recorded. Pending and uncertain results remain distinct from completion. The operator sees a read-only number-operation card and issue context; there is no acquisition or cancellation control on the customer's behalf.
