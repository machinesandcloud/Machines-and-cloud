## What competitor evidence changes

Official public pages checked October 22, 2026. These are documented offerings, not trials we completed or proof of comparative performance.

- [Goodcall FAQ](https://www.goodcall.com/faq) documents a free demo before adding a number, practice then deliberate publishing, carrier-controlled conditional forwarding, and separate account members versus transfer contacts. **Implication:** preserve the free first useful test, explain the exact remaining customer-controlled action, and avoid treating all connection work as manual setup. Our real first-result acceptance is still missing.
- [Jobber Home](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/home/) describes a goal-based setup guide and a daily view of active, overdue and completed appointments. **Implication:** the portal's next evolution should connect setup to actual owner work and meaningful completion, rather than add decorative statistics. We already have a guide and navigation; another tour would duplicate those capabilities.
- [Intercom Inbox](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/6258745-the-inbox-explained), dated September 17, 2026, documents open/closed/snoozed states, assignment and conversation context. **Implication:** place action ownership and state beside the request. We do not need its whole enterprise inbox to distinguish “I took this” from “I spoke to the customer.”
- [Rosie](https://heyrosie.com/) markets website/profile-based training, shared knowledge for calls and chat, calendar booking and mobile notifications. **Implication:** website-assisted draft creation is a reasonable later reduction in typing, with facts sourced, editable and explicitly reviewed. We currently build from owner answers; do not describe automatic website training as shipped or import unsafe/unverified instructions into live rules.

Existing operator evidence in [stakeholder research](../RESEARCH-stakeholder-forums.md) is also relevant: F1 describes phone-versus-visit confusion; F2/F3 describe organizing existing work and respecting crew availability; F9 asks for a basic small-team tool. These are specific public anecdotes with recorded limitations, not measured demand or customer validation. They support the focused commitment and availability work above, rather than a full CRM/FSM rebuild.

## PO decision

The independent [UI/UX review](UIUX-NEXT-WORK-2026-10-22.md) and PO agree that reliable editing is the immediate P0 repair: a customer must be able to correct the business facts and trust the save result before we ask them to approve or activate those facts. This is a focused reliability repair in the existing design. Independent offline QA reproduced both defects against published assets and actual isolated handlers, with zero physical requests and stable source. No actual production customer loss is claimed. The current joint delivery scope is [Sprint 19](SPRINT19.md); the application remains unchanged by this review.

First fix **reliable editing and honest save recovery** in the existing workspace. Run production signup/return acceptance preparation in parallel; it remains a launch gate. Then continue the exact-tenant connection controls and real service proof, while delivering explicit commitment ownership and honest contact completion as the next small customer feature. UI/UX should also make the first customer conversation/install path obvious. Continue research, but require a concrete owner job and measurable result before adding a feature.

Paid billing, general Enterprise delivery, unrestricted CRM integrations, waitlist blasts, native mobile apps and paid specialist checkout do not outrank these outcomes. Standard supported automation stays free to set up. Optional specialist help remains an unlaunched proposal until real fulfillment, scope, price and permissions exist; platform defects are not paid setup work.

The next PO acceptance is an observed owner/customer result with retained failure evidence. Neither another research document nor a passing local helper suite closes it.
