## Recommended decision

1. **Keep free workspace setup while proving the delivery workflow.** Do not charge for inactive phone/SMS/integrations or convert a free workspace into a paid trial. Make the current web-chat allowance and shared availability visible before activation. Free setup is not an unlimited-free-service promise.
2. **Use two future price hypotheses: $99 Coverage and $249 Follow-through.** These are price points to validate, not approved launch packages or willingness-to-pay evidence. $99 needs dependable light/overflow coverage. $249 must earn its premium through observed owner follow-through and customer outcomes; listing future follow-up features is insufficient. Published Spanish/chat availability elsewhere already makes those weak standalone differentiators.
3. **Hold the $449 Pro selling proposition.** Do not expand its5,000-text/1,500-minute promise or use it as the default upgrade. It discounts the usage where our variable costs are greatest; extra crews, integrations and priority support cannot justify it until actually delivered. The current preference can remain uncharged until an explicit package decision reconciles every surface.
4. **Choose final allowances only after the cost gate passes.** The150/400/800-minute and250/750/1,500-outgoing-segment comparison is a cost-containment example, not the selected offer. Even it falls short of a proposed60% service-contribution gate at an assumed $0.18/minute in the present model. Do not publish smaller units under an unchanged “texts” label or promise the same service at lower cost without explaining the change.
5. **Replace the unsupported overage assumptions before paid launch.** $0.49 per extra voice minute and $0.05 per outgoing SMS segment are candidates to test, not approved or proven profitable rates. The voice rate’s modeled marginal contribution falls from60.37% at $0.18 cost/minute to35.88% at $0.30; the segment rate also needs inbound, owner-alert and AI reserves. Existing $0.35/minute and $0.03/text remain informational preferences while billing is off. Define length, rounding, approval/spend caps and which traffic is included before any charge.

## What our cost model establishes

[Architect’s cost report](PRICING-COSTS-2026-10-22.md) traces the actual stack: Vapi orchestration, default Sonnet voice model, Deepgram transcription, unpinned voice generation and Twilio; typed web/SMS uses a separate Opus loop. We have no accepted live paid-tenant cost receipt for the complete configured service. Provider list prices are sourced, but actual utilization, tokens, voice configuration, incoming texts, support and fixed-cost allocations remain assumptions.

The planning case uses $0.18/all-in voice minute, two outgoing segments per counted application text, current sourced carrier-rate examples, plus explicitly assumed incoming/owner traffic and AI reply mix. It allocates $15/month service+$5 web-AI reserve and2.9%+$0.30 payment processing; human support is separate. It excludes extra Stripe Billing, tax, acquisition, one-time registration and company overhead. Equal utilization of both allowances is a sensitivity, not observed behavior. Voice cost applies a simplifying 1:1 provider-cost-minute versus plan-minute proxy; actual per-call rounding, elapsed duration and transferred legs still require reconciliation. The $5 web reserve is an allocation assumption, not an enforced AI spend cap.

| Current preference, both allowances fully used | Modeled monthly service cost including payment, before support | Service contribution margin | Including $15 support reserve |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Starter $99 | $70.52 | 28.77% | 13.62% |
| Growth $249 | $196.56 | 21.06% | 15.04% |
| Pro $449 | $506.77 | −12.87% | −16.21% |

These are conditional estimates, not losses observed on invoices. Lower utilization changes the result substantially: at50% planning usage, contribution before support is52.68%/55.00%/39.86%. Service contribution includes payment fees; it is not company profit or accounting gross margin.

The text unit is material. [Twilio bills segments](https://www.twilio.com/docs/glossary/what-sms-character-limit), while our informational meter counts outgoing application sends. Long/non-GSM content can use several segments; inbound replies and owner messages also cost money. At an explicitly assumed5,000 fresh input tokens+150 output tokens, current Opus list rates imply $0.023 for one model request. Adding one $0.0128 outbound segment is already $0.0358 before inbound traffic or additional tool rounds. This is why $0.03/application-text overage cannot be approved merely from the carrier’s headline rate.

[Interactive scenario worksheet](../scratchpad/pricing-review.html) lets a reviewer switch voice-cost band,25/50/100% allowance use and optional support. It runs locally without tracking, account changes or provider requests. All assumptions are visible; the revised segment-based allowances are explicitly distinct from the current application-message bundle.

## Required work before charging

[QA’s pricing audit](PRICING-AUDIT-2026-10-22.md) records exact source evidence and acceptance cases. Its important returns are:

- **One consistent offer:** six older acquisition pages and the About page retain free-week/private-phone-pilot/live-in-seven-days claims. Current features such as CSV export and editable rules should not be represented as paid-only upgrades. Correct visible copy and JSON-LD together when implementing the selected offer; preserve the SEO pages.
- **An honest unit and allowance contract:** specify aggregate elapsed versus per-call rounded minutes, SMS logical messages versus carrier segments, incoming/owner/consent/maintenance traffic, web AI’s per-tenant and shared caps, reset period, failed attempts, overage consent and spend protection. A plan preference currently increases none of the shared AI allowances.
- **A trustworthy ledger:** deduplicate by provider call/message identity, retain concurrent events, fail closed on storage uncertainty, reconcile provider receipts and align monthly usage with the actual subscription period. Disabled/fake sends and replayed callbacks must never become charges.
- **Correct payment mapping:** reject missing/mismatched provider price IDs, maintain idempotent subscription events, preserve new-workspace billing gates, and define cancellation/renewal/refund/overage/tax presentation. The current parked legacy path is not an invoice-ready implementation.
- **A measured economic gate:** agree a contribution target (60% minimum/65% preferred are proposed internal hypotheses, not industry requirements), then validate full-use and adverse segmentation/transfer/support cases. Record the active voice configuration and real provider cost breakdowns in an independently authorized operational pass; reconcile every cost category and fix the offer if it fails. Do not assume unused allowances will subsidize a heavy user.
- **Customer value acceptance:** prepare a consented small-shop cohort with written uncharged proposals and defined coverage; a later separately approved paid pilot measures accepted prices, service cost, support time, safe completed handoffs and renewal intent. This review authorizes no outreach or paid experiment. Do not measure willingness to pay by a survey compliment or promise guaranteed recovered revenue.

Owner assignments: PO owns offer/value validation and removes unsupported claims; PM sequences the price/unit decision and acceptance gates; Architect owns the cost/ledger contract; Engineer implements approved entitlements/metering/copy; QA verifies real billing outcomes and failure paths; OM owns spend/receipts/recovery and service continuity. Billing stays off until the complete paid-service gate is accepted. Current published and local chatbot stages remain as recorded in PRODUCT-STATUS and SALES-DESK.
