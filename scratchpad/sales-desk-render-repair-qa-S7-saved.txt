Front desk
Track every inbound contact for one to two normal weeks, then look for where they stall. Here's a simple way to do it:

**1. Log every contact**
Use a spreadsheet or paper sheet with one row per call, form, text or lead:

| Date/time | Channel | Answered live? | Time to first response | Outcome | Reason if lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tue 7:40am | Phone | No, voicemail | 2 hrs | Booked elsewhere | Called back too late |

- **Channel:** phone, website form, text, lead platform or walk-in
- **Outcome:** booked, quoted, callback, no answer, or went elsewhere

**2. Pull records you already have**
- **Phone:** Your phone provider's call log shows missed calls, calls that hung up while waiting, and calls that went to voicemail. Your receptionist can't see the calls they never picked up.
- **Website forms and lead platforms:** Check the time each one came in against the time someone first replied.

**3. Sort the lost and slow contacts by when they happened**
Problems usually bunch up around:
- Before opening, after closing and weekends
- Lunch or breaks
- Busy stretches when your receptionist is already on a call
- Slow callbacks on voicemails and forms

**4. Check what happens after the first contact**
Sometimes intake is fine and the holdup is quoting, scheduling or not having enough technicians. If calls get answered but jobs still don't get booked, the problem is further along.

**5. Read the results**
- **Lost contacts bunch up in certain hours:** You have a coverage gap.
- **Contacts get answered but replies are slow:** You have a follow-up problem.
- **Contacts get answered and replied to fast, but jobs aren't booked:** Look at pricing, quoting or capacity, not how calls are answered.
- **Nothing much is lost:** Your current setup is working, and you don't need to change it.
