How to plan a useful first test

Choose one task, gather representative examples and decide what a useful result looks like. Test mistakes and missing information as carefully as successful cases.

1. Define a useful result

Name the person who uses the result and the time, errors or rework you want to reduce.

2. Choose the information

Gather a small set of approved records and examples the team can check.

3. Test with the team

Compare the result with the current process. Record corrections and decide together what is worth taking further.

Information to check before testing

  • SIS grades
  • LMS engagement
  • Application documents
  • OCR
  • Service catalog
  • KB
  • Document corpus
  • permissions

Questions to ask before choosing a tool

How can an education team choose a useful first task?

Compare recurring questions or document tasks that staff can review against current practice. Include accessibility, privacy and the effect of errors on students when deciding what to test.

How should schools handle AI recommendations that affect students?

Use human review, preserve evidence and explanations, and avoid autonomous decisions on high-stakes student outcomes.

Which education task are you exploring?

Choose one recurring student question or document task. Bring its sources and the review decisions your staff make today.
